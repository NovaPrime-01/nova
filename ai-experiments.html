01 — What & why

Experiments, not claims

AI experiments here are practical demonstrations or clearly identified experimental concepts. Each one states its name, purpose, category, how it works, the technology involved, its current status and its known limitations. Status labels are used strictly: Functional means it works today with a real model behind it; Demonstration means a working interface with no AI model called; Concept means an idea that has not been built. No performance measurements are claimed, and no prototype is presented as independently tested.

Develop and refine creative prompts for design, writing, video, code and image workflows. This is a local prompt-structuring tool — no AI model is called and no data leaves your browser. It organises your idea into role, context, constraints and output format so you can paste the result into any AI workflow you already use.

How it works — a local template engine combines your idea with task-specific structure: role, context, constraints and output format. Known limitations: it organises your thinking, it does not generate content or call a model. Privacy: no network requests, no storage, no tracking.

03 — Honesty & security

No keys, no fake responses

This site is fully static: there is no backend and no API key in any client-side file, bundle or public environment variable. That is why the interactive tool above is explicitly labelled as a demonstration, and why model-backed ideas stay labelled Concept until a secure server-side integration exists. When a real model is eventually wired in, requests will run through a server-side endpoint, secrets will stay server-side, users will be told their input is being sent, and failure states will show honest error messages instead of simulated success.
